Image 2: Columbia University Class of 1877, Honoring James R. Priest. Photograph, 1877. Columbia University Archives, New York.

The calling card is a form that is historically associated with bourgeois social culture as a formal notice. Adrian Piper’s work repurposes this form to describe an anticipated encounter in which a racist remark is made in a place where one believed themselves to be among their own. The last line, “I regret any discomfort my presence is causing you […] ”,  presents the mannerisms of this space while simultaneously showcasing that the state of waiting for such a remark is perpetual. It is placed alongside a 1877 Columbia class photograph honoring James R. Priest, one of the university’s early Black graduates. Priest is surrounded by an institution that has “extended” his entry, and the photograph holds the question of what that belonging costs.
